As a Global Lead Student representing North America at the COP16, I had the opportunity to advocate for youth perspectives on biodiversity and environmental sustainability at an international level. Selected to represent children and youth globally, I collaborated with students, educators, and environmental leaders from around the world to contribute to discussions surrounding the future of biodiversity conservation and sustainable development. During the conference, I presented the Global Child Biodiversity Declaration in several panels, a document developed through the contributions of more than 600 children across 24 countries. The declaration highlighted youth priorities and recommendations regarding biodiversity protection, climate resilience, and environmental education. I had the privilege of sharing these perspectives directly with influential international leaders, including representatives from the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), the Convention on Biological Diversity, and the Canadian Wildlife Federation.

Additionally, I had the opportunity to lead a panel, covering youth perspectives on transformative education highlighting success, challenges, hopes and aspirations. Beyond policy discussions, COP16 allowed me to engage with diverse global perspectives and better understand the intersection between environmental science, policy, education, and community action. Collaborating with delegates from different countries strengthened my communication, leadership, and cross-cultural collaboration skills while reinforcing the importance of youth involvement in global decision-making processes. My experience at COP16 further deepened my commitment to environmental advocacy and sustainable innovation. It inspired me to continue pursuing initiatives that combine science, technology, and community engagement to address environmental challenges and create meaningful, long-term impact.
